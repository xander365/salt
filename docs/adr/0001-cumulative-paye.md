# Cumulative PAYE against year-to-date, not annualised months

Namibian PAYE uses annual progressive bands but is withheld each period, so a per-period figure has to be derived. Salt calculates PAYE cumulatively: tax owed on year-to-date taxable remuneration, less PAYE already withheld this tax year. We rejected annualising the current period (`month × 12 ÷ 12`), which most local payroll vendors use.

**This is Salt policy, not Namibian law.** No prescribed deduction table or method could be found published. Schedule 2 of the Income Tax Act anticipates tables and a manner of application prescribed by the Minister; it does not define this formula. The choice is real and substantive — Sage ships two methods for Namibia, Normal Tax (annualise the current period) and Average Tax (year-to-date income based on time worked), and Salt implements neither of them but a third shape. Until NamRA confirms a method, per-period PAYE results are `salt_policy_*` tests, never `statutory_*` ones, and `docs/domain/statutory-conformance.md` carries the open question as SC-OPEN-1.

Cumulative costs more up front — every calculation needs a `YearToDateContext`, and employers joining mid-year must supply opening balances — but it handles irregular remuneration correctly without special cases, and it is self-correcting: a fixed earlier period is absorbed by the next calculation rather than cascading through every later one. That self-correction is what makes ADR-0002 cheap.

**The mechanism, precisely** (this is the part a first implementation got wrong): tax owed is not simply the annual band table applied to year-to-date taxable remuneration outright. The annual band *thresholds* are first scaled to `periods_elapsed_inclusive / 12` — periods_elapsed_inclusive being the periods elapsed before this one, plus this one — and the (un-annualised) year-to-date taxable amount is taxed against those scaled thresholds. At period 12 the thresholds equal the full annual table, so every employer reconciles to the standard annual calculation over exactly 12 periods. Earlier in the year the same cumulative income can land in a different band than it would later on — that is intentional, not a bug: it is what lets a large one-off amount get taxed appropriately in the period it is paid, rather than waiting for December to "catch up." This is scaling the *thresholds*, never the income — annualising the income itself (`ytd × 12 / periods_elapsed`) is the rejected approach above.

**`PeriodsElapsed` counts position in the tax year, not periods the Employment has been paid.** This was challenged on the belief that it under-taxes a new starter, and it does not. Someone starting in October on N$25,000 a month earns N$125,000 in the tax year against a true annual liability of N$4,500. In October the thresholds scale to 8/12, N$25,000 falls below the scaled zero-tax band, and PAYE is nil — but by February the thresholds are whole and the full N$4,500 has been withheld. Counting *worked* periods instead would scale October to 1/12 and over-withhold against the liability the employee actually owes. Tax-year position is what makes the method reconcile, and it is kept.

The genuine risk is a different one, and it is closed below: an employee who already earned taxable remuneration **with another employer** in the same tax year. Salt records that fact and refuses; it does not calculate around it.

## Consequences

- `YearToDateContext` (TaxYear, prior taxable remuneration, prior PAYE, periods elapsed) is a required part of every `PayrollInput`, never `Option` — the first period of adoption passes explicit zeros. There is therefore no "missing year-to-date context" runtime state for `calculate` to refuse; that refusal is only reachable if a caller could omit the context entirely, which the type does not allow.
- `YearToDateContext` additionally carries an explicit three-way fact about **prior employment with another Employer in this tax year**: `None` (confirmed: none), `Some(taxable, paye)` (figures from the employee's tax certificate), or `Unknown`. `None` proceeds. `Unknown` makes `calculate` refuse — zero must not be able to mean "nobody asked", and that conflation, not the meaning of `PeriodsElapsed`, is how a mid-year joiner gets materially under-taxed. `Some(..)` **also refuses**: how a new employer must treat those figures, and whether a NamRA directive or certificate is required first, is unresolved (SC-OPEN-4), and consuming them would turn an open question into shipped payroll behaviour. The figures are carried into the typed error rather than discarded, so nothing has to be re-gathered once the treatment is confirmed. This is not a policy decision under an unprescribed method like the rest of this ADR — there is no policy, there is a refusal.
- An `OpeningBalance` per Employment per TaxYear is required for any employer starting mid-year. This is a **different fact** from prior employment and stays fully supported: it is prior year-to-date payroll for the *same* Employment and Employer, from before Salt. The two must never be conflated.
- Year-to-date is always summed from live, non-reversed `FinalizedPayroll` records plus the `OpeningBalance`. It is never stored as a running total.
- If a correction lowers a prior period's figures enough that recalculated year-to-date liability comes out *below* what the (corrected) context says was already withheld, `calculate` refuses with a typed error rather than returning a refund or clamping to zero PAYE. Refund handling is not modeled anywhere in this domain yet, and clamping would silently mask what is either a genuine over-withholding event needing human review or a data-entry inconsistency in the supplied `YearToDateContext` (INV-012). This can be revisited if refunds become a real requirement, but that is a new decision, not an extension of this one.
